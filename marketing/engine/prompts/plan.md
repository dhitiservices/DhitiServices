# How to choose topics

Pick exactly the requested number of topics, plus the requested number of alternates. Each topic must pass all five tests:

1. **Buyer relevance.** It addresses a real, current pain for a specific buyer (travel agency, TMC, tour operator, OTA, DMC, hotel). Prefer pains supported by this week's research brief.
2. **Service fit.** The fix involves steady, rule-based, checkable work that Dhiti's teams do (back office, accounts and finance support, reconciliation, data and rate loading, refunds, ADMs, commissions, reporting, customer and sales support, quality and audit). The closing call to action must feel like the obvious next step, not a bolt-on.
3. **No duplication.** It must not repeat or closely overlap an existing post or a recently planned topic. A new angle on a neighbouring subject is fine only if the primary keyword and the reader's question are clearly different. When in doubt, choose something else.
4. **Search intent.** The primary keyword is a phrase a real buyer types into Google when they have this problem (2 to 5 words, lowercase, no brand names, for example "adm dispute process", "hotel commission tracking", "gst on tour packages"). Use web search sparingly to sanity-check that the phrase is used and that results are guides rather than products. Avoid keywords dominated by government or airline pages the reader needs instead of us, and avoid purely news queries that will be stale in a month.
5. **Timeliness or evergreen value.** Either the brief gives a reason this week, or it is a durable how-to that buyers search for all year. Ideally both.

Balance the set: vary buyer types (not all hotel, not all TMC), vary service lines, and do not pick two topics that would cannibalise each other.

# Fields

- `title`: a working headline in the house style, plain and specific, often naming the problem and the outcome ("Agency debit memos: how travel agencies stop paying for avoidable mistakes"). 50 to 75 characters is ideal. No clickbait, no question marks unless it is a genuine question, no colons stacked.
- `angle`: 2 to 3 sentences on the specific argument the post will make and the scenario it will open with. This is the brief the writer follows, so make it sharp.
- `primary_keyword`, `secondary_keywords` (3 to 6 related phrases).
- `audience`: the specific reader.
- `category`: exactly one of the allowed categories you are given.
- `rationale`: why this topic, why this week, which research items support it (mention them), and how the CTA to Dhiti will work.
- `priority`: 1 is the strongest topic; it will be published first. Use each number once.
- `research_refs`: the source URLs from the brief that the writer should start from.
