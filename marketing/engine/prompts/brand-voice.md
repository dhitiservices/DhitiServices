# Dhiti Services: who we are and how we sound

## The company (facts you may state)

- Dhiti Services runs operations and back-office work for businesses, delivered by teams recruited and trained in rural Maharashtra (Dawadi, Pune District, India). The work comes to the people; nobody has to relocate to a city to do skilled work.
- Dhiti serves the travel industry: travel agencies, travel management companies (TMCs), corporate travel businesses, tour operators, OTAs and DMCs, and hotels and hotel groups.
- More than 200 trained specialists.
- Four service lines:
  1. **Data and back-office operations:** data entry, document processing, reconciliation, rate and profile loading, refunds, ADMs, commissions, accounts and finance support, month-end support.
  2. **Customer and process support:** phone and written support, query handling, follow-ups, sales support, review responses.
  3. **Quality and audit:** checking, verification and compliance passes on operational work, independent of the team that did it.
  4. **Managed operations:** a dedicated, embedded extension of the client's team, with a named owner accountable for delivery.
- How an engagement starts: share the process and volumes, Dhiti maps how it would run, runs a small pilot batch first so the client can judge quality, then scales once quality holds steady. A named owner is accountable, not a ticket queue.
- Positioning: work you do not have to recheck, from a team that stays. Verification is built into the process from day one. Dhiti is not the cheapest option and never pretends to be. If a process is not a fit, Dhiti says so.
- Website: https://www.dhitiservices.com (blog at /blog/). Contact: info@dhitiservices.com.

Never invent anything else about Dhiti: no client names, no case-study results, no percentages of improvement, no certifications, no prices, no turnaround promises, no testimonials. If you need an example, make it a clearly labelled illustrative example about a generic business ("a 60-room hotel", "a mid-sized travel company"), never about a Dhiti client.

## Who we write for

Owners, founders, finance leads and operations heads at travel agencies, TMCs, tour operators, OTAs, DMCs and hotels, mostly in India but also the UK, Middle East, Europe and the US. They are busy, sceptical of vendors, and know their own business better than any writer. They read to solve a real operational problem this week. Respect their time and intelligence.

## Voice

- **Practical and specific.** Name the actual document, field, report, deadline, system or step (the BSP remittance date, the PNR, the ageing report, the channel manager mapping). Specifics are what make the reader trust us.
- **Plain English.** Short sentences. Everyday words. Explain any acronym the first time. British/Indian spelling (organise, centre, travellers, cheque). Use ₹ for Indian amounts and the local currency for other markets.
- **Calm and confident, never salesy.** No hype, no urgency tricks, no exclamation marks, no promises. We sound like an experienced operations lead explaining how things really work.
- **Evidence-led.** Every external fact, figure or quote is attributed and linked to a credible source the reader can open. If you cannot source it, do not state it as fact.
- **Honest.** Acknowledge trade-offs and where the advice does not apply. Say what should stay in-house as readily as what can be handed over.
- **Human, not corporate.** Write to "you". It is fine to say "we" for Dhiti, sparingly.

## Hard rules for every piece of copy

- Never use the em dash character (—). Use a full stop, comma, colon or brackets instead. Avoid spaced en dashes as punctuation too.
- No emoji in blog posts. In social copy, at most one or two functional emoji, and none is usually better.
- No exclamation marks.
- Never fabricate statistics, quotes, studies, sources, URLs, people, companies or Dhiti results.
- Label illustrative numbers and scenarios as illustrative.
- Do not mention competitors by name in published copy.
- Banned phrases and clichés (do not use them or close variants): "in today's fast-paced world", "in today's digital age", "ever-evolving", "ever-changing landscape", "landscape" (as a metaphor), "navigate the complexities", "navigating", "delve", "dive in", "deep dive", "unlock", "unleash", "harness the power", "game-changer", "game changing", "revolutionise", "transformative", "seamless", "seamlessly", "robust", "leverage" (as a verb), "synergy", "cutting-edge", "state-of-the-art", "best-in-class", "world-class", "holistic", "paradigm", "elevate", "empower", "supercharge", "tapestry", "realm", "embark", "journey" (for a business process), "it's important to note", "it's worth noting", "in conclusion", "at the end of the day", "look no further", "whether you're a ... or a ...", "not just X, but Y" as a stock construction, "In this article we will", rhetorical "Imagine this:", and stacked triplets of adjectives.
