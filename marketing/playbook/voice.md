# Human voice: how every Dhiti word must sound (owner rule, binding)

Applies to everything: blog, carousel slides, graphics, reel on-screen text, voiceover scripts, LinkedIn captions and first comments.
The owner's bar: it must read like a sharp, experienced travel-operations person wrote it by hand. Never generic, never "AI-sounding".
The banned-phrase lint (quality-bar.md section 5) catches words. This file covers what a lint cannot.

## Write like an operator, not a marketer
- Start from a real moment: a person, a desk, a time, a document. "It's the 4th of the month and the client's finance team has sent a list of eleven flights." Not "Managing GST is challenging for many businesses."
- Say one specific thing per paragraph. Concrete nouns (invoice, GSTIN, booking profile, ADM, rooming list), real numbers with sources, real steps someone can do on Monday.
- Have a point of view. Say what you would do and why, and what most teams get wrong. Disagree with common advice when the facts support it.
- Show the work: the column names, the check, the message to send, the exact field. Readers trust detail.
- Write for one reader you can picture (a TMC finance head, an agency owner, a hotel revenue manager), in "you" and "we".

## Rhythm and sound
- Mix sentence lengths. A short one lands a point. A longer one carries the reason, the condition and the consequence in one breath.
- Plain words: "use" not "utilise", "help" not "facilitate", "about" not "approximately", "check" not "ensure".
- No AI patterns: no lists of three adjectives, no "not just X but Y", no rhetorical question openers, no summary paragraph that repeats the post, no "Here's why/how:" lead-ins, no colon-heavy headings on every section, no every-paragraph-the-same-length.
- No hype and no filler adverbs (truly, really, incredibly, seamlessly). Confidence comes from facts.
- Headings say the useful thing ("Match every ticket before the client files"), not a label ("Reconciliation Process").
- Read it aloud. If a sentence sounds like a brochure or like nobody would say it across a desk, rewrite it.

## Short formats (slides, graphics, reels, captions)
- One idea per slide or frame. Every line earns its place; cut until it hurts, then cut one more word.
- Hooks are specific and a little surprising: a number, a scene, a mistake. "11 flights. ₹0 tax credit." beats "Don't lose your GST credit!"
- Voiceover sounds spoken: contractions, short clauses, natural pauses, no tongue-twisters, no lists of numbers read aloud.
- LinkedIn caption: talk like a founder to peers. First two lines make a busy operator stop. No "Excited to share", no emoji walls, max 3 hashtags at the end.

## The human edit pass (mandatory before saving anything)
1. Delete the first paragraph and see if the piece is better. If yes, keep it deleted.
2. Underline every sentence that could appear in any company's blog. Rewrite each one with a Dhiti-specific detail or remove it.
3. Check every claim has its source in claims.json (accuracy beats style, always).
4. Vary at least three sentence openings that start the same way.
5. Read the hook, headings and CTA alone: they must still make sense and sound like a person.
6. Run lint.sh. Fix everything it prints.
