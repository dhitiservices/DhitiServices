# SEO: every blog must earn rankings and organic traffic (owner rule, binding)

Read with planning.md, blog-writing.md and voice.md. Accuracy and human voice come first; SEO never justifies a weaker or less true sentence.

## 1. Pick topics people actually search
- Every topic targets ONE primary keyword with real search demand (Google suggestions, "People also ask", related searches, several ranking pages) plus 3-6 secondary/long-tail variants. Prefer specific, buyer-intent long-tail phrases a small site can win ("ADM dispute process for travel agencies", "hotel rate parity check checklist") over broad head terms.
- Check the live SERP before writing: what intent wins (guide, checklist, definition, comparison), what the top 5 cover, and what they miss. Our post must be the most specific, most useful answer for that intent. If the top results are tools, portals or government pages for a navigational query, pick another keyword.
- Never target a keyword an existing Dhiti post already targets (cannibalisation). Check content/blog and context.recent_topics.

## 2. Topic clusters (six services x four audiences)
- Build clusters around the six services (quality-bar.md). Each new post links to 2-4 related existing posts with descriptive anchor text (not "click here"), and names the matching service naturally in the CTA section.
- When a new post fits an older post, the revisions run may add one natural link from the older post to the new one (only if the older post is not locked/approved-pending and the sentence stays true).

## 3. On-page checklist (check-blog enforces part of this)
- Title 50-60 characters ideal (max 80): primary keyword near the start, a clear benefit or number, no clickbait. Description 140-155 characters: who it is for + what they get, includes the keyword.
- Slug: 3-6 words from the primary keyword. H1 = title. Primary keyword in the first 100 words and in one H2. H2s answer real sub-questions (use People also ask wording where natural).
- A 40-60 word direct answer to the main question early in the post (featured-snippet paragraph), then depth.
- Steps as numbered lists, comparisons as tables, so Google can show them.
- "## Frequently asked questions" section with 3-5 "### Question?" headings and short, complete answers. The site turns this into FAQ structured data automatically.
- Cover image: descriptive file name from the slug, alt text that describes the image and context (not keyword stuffing).
- 1,500-2,200 words only when the topic needs it; no padding. Freshness: absolute dates, "As of <month year>" where rules change.
- E-E-A-T: show first-hand operating detail, cite primary sources (claims ledger), name Dhiti's real process; no invented credentials or client results.

## 4. After publishing
- The LinkedIn post links to the blog in the first comment (UTM tagged) to bring early visits.
- Every 4 weeks the planning run lists posts older than 6 months whose rules or numbers may have changed and queues a refresh (re-verify claims, update the "As of" date and the `updated` frontmatter date, which the site uses as dateModified).
